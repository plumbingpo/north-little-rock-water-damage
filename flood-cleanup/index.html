Maintenance Tips for Flood Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • Roof runoff should leave the downspout far enough out that it cannot soak the foundation
  • Store valuables and important papers off the basement floor in waterproof containers
  • Make sure the ground slopes away from your foundation on every side
  • Have a backwater valve fitted to the sewer line so sewage cannot flow back inside
  • Coat basement walls with waterproofing and seal any cracks in the foundation

Why Hire a Professional for Flood Cleanup?

There is much more to a flood cleanup than removing the water you can see. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals and other contaminants that are serious health hazards. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. Nothing else reliably heads off secondary damage, and that damage can run thousands more to remediate, so we dry by the numbers.

When to Call for Flood Cleanup in North Little Rock

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood
  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
  • Receding water that leaves muddy residue and debris behind
  • Ground so saturated that puddles sit against the foundation walls
  • Flooring that squelches when you walk on it and walls damp to the touch
  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through
  • Standing water anywhere on your property, whatever the source
