Hardwood Floor Water Damage in North Little Rock

Wood floors react to water quickly. As the wood absorbs water along its edges and bottom face, it expands, crowds the neighboring boards and cups, leaving the center lower than the sides. That does not mean the floor is lost. Solid hardwood that is dried in the first few days often flattens back out. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

Drying mats are how our team handles wet wood floors. Each mat is sealed against the floor and hooked to a vacuum unit, which pulls moisture upward through seams and boards as dehumidifiers dry the air in the room. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. A cupped floor sanded early ends up crowned afterward.

Signs to Look For

  • Nail heads and seams ringed by dark staining
  • Boards lifting off the subfloor or tenting upward at the seams
  • Crowning, meaning the middle of each board bulges above its edges
  • A musty smell rising from the floor with nothing visible on top
  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat
  • Cupping, where each board's edges rise above its middle
The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Most of the water ends up beneath the boards, having run down through the joints. The surface may wipe dry while the underside stays soaked.

Hours 24 to 48

Cupping Starts

Swelling sets in and board edges lift. You might notice haze in the topcoat. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Boards Start Lifting

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. Edges get crushed together, nails work loose and parts of the floor may start rising from the subfloor.

After a week

Beyond Drying

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. At this stage most of the wet area needs to be replaced rather than dried.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Wet Subfloor

The subfloor can hold moisture long after the boards feel dry. If it is not dried too, the floor will cup again or grow mold from underneath.

Drying Too Fast

When the top of the wood dries much quicker than the inside, stress builds in the board. The result can be cracks, splits and permanent gaps between boards.

Damage Beyond the Room

Water under a floor travels. Cabinets and wall plates do not stop it, so it may enter adjacent rooms or come out at the ceiling one floor down.

Rusting Fasteners

Staples and flooring nails corrode when moisture lingers, and their grip weakens. It can leave black staining around each fastener that goes deeper than a sanding reaches.

What We Do

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Take Baseline Readings

Our team meters the wet boards, the subfloor under them and a dry area of floor to compare against. The comparison shows how far down moisture reached and how likely the floor is to recover.

2

Extract Standing Water

Rugs and furnishings come off the affected boards while pooled water is vacuumed up at once. The faster water stops feeding the seams, the less the boards swell.

3

Set Drying Mats

We tape drying mats to the floor and attach them to a vacuum system, which draws moisture upward through the wood. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Monitor and Advise

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.
